The results demonstrate a systematic widening of the performance gap, particularly among men and young athletes. This increase in the performance range correlates directly with the 11.9\% decline in German Athletics Association (DLV) membership and the massive 26.6\% loss in the 27- to 60-year-old age group \citep{LeichtathletikPortalDownloads}. This is the largest group of coaches, and their decline is particularly noticeable in technical disciplines, which rely on more intensive coaching.

Our analysis of the years during the pandemic also reveals a difference between disciplines. Running disciplines improved thanks to carbon technology \citep{willwacherDoesAdvancedFootwear2023} and uninterrupted training blocks \citep{venkatesanRunningPerformanceCOVID192022}. Training facility closures led to a measurable drop in performance in the technical disciplines. Women, on the other hand, show a catching-up effect, suggesting that professionalization is not yet complete.

The changed distribution of resources has further intensified this polarization. National team athletes are financially secure through centralized funding. The extended elite relies on external support, which was severely restricted during the COVID-19 pandemic \citep{breuerOekonomischenAuswirkungenCOVID19}. As a result, this extended elite has been lost. This lack of internal competition weakens international competitiveness in the long term, true to the adage "competition fosters excellence."

The analysis is limited to the top 35 athletes and thus represents the extended elite, but not grassroots sports. This should be further investigated in the future to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the structural changes. The trend toward polarization could explain the lack of medals at international championships. Future focus should be on strengthening the extended elite and coach training to revitalize competition, which will also benefit elite athletes.
